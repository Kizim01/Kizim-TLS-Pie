Rev 3.0 — fitting the 3S BMS

How the NLY-3C-V3.0 protection board joins the 3S12P pack and the rest of the rig, and the two things it changes everywhere else. This closes the blocker that made a full pack look flat and caused every brownout.

Rev3.0 Supersedes2.0 · 2026-08-09 Date2026-08-11 StatusON HOLD — see above ERC0 errors
⛔ Stop — do not fit this board yet. The pack's topology is unverified.

Everything on this page assumes the pack is 3S. That assumption is now in doubt: the pack is 12 cells in 4 rows of 3, not the 36 the record said. Physical layout does not settle topology — it depends where the nickel strips run — but “4 rows” is exactly what 4S3P looks like, and in that case the 4S board already fitted was correct all along.

At the measured 12.22 V, 3S means 4.07 V/cell — nearly full, so a BMS refusing to conduct must be faulty for the pack. 4S means 3.05 V/cell — genuinely low, and one weak group easily sits under the cutoff. Both explain the ~0.55 V body-diode reading equally well. One says the BMS is wrong; the other says it was right and doing its job.

The one measurement that settles it — on the OLD 4S board, between B3+ and B4+:
~0 V → no fourth cell → 3S, and this page stands.
~3 V → there is a fourth cell → 4S, and this board must not go on.

If that board is already off, walk the meter across each cell-group junction from pack negative instead: three steps of ~4.07 V = 3S, four steps of ~3.05 V = 4S. Corroborate without opening anything by counting the balance conductors (5 = 4S, 4 = 3S) and by the charger's open-circuit voltage (16.8 V = 4S, 12.6 V = 3S).

Why this is a hard stop rather than a caveat: a 3S board on a 4S pack puts about 16.8 V on a B3+ input rated for three cells, which destroys the IC — and leaves the fourth cell with no protection at all, no over-discharge floor and no over-charge ceiling. If the pack measures 4S the right action is to charge it with a 16.8 V charger and find the weak group, not to change the BMS.

It was not faulty — with no fourth cell it latched under-voltage permanently and passed current through the MOSFET body diodes, which is the ~0.55 V that was measured across it. Through a body diode you lose about 0.6 V and can pass only a trickle: fine at idle, collapsing the instant the motor pulls. That is what caused both brownouts and the reboot mid-move, and what made a 30 Ah pack look empty. Bridging its top balance taps does not help; the IC still sees a 0 V cell.

01 Three measurements before anything is connected

  1. The charger, open circuit, with nothing attached. A 3S Li-ion pack charges to 12.6 V. If it reads 13.8–14.4 V it is a lead-acid charger and must not go near this pack — it would push 4.6–4.8 V per cell. With the old BMS latched off, nothing has been protecting these cells, so this is not a formality.
  2. The cell-group voltages, at the pack — and count them. This is also the test in the stop notice above: three groups of ~4.07 V confirms 3S and unblocks this page; four groups of ~3.05 V means 4S and none of it applies. Once counted, each group should sit within about 50 mV of the others. A cheap board balances slowly or not at all, so bring them together by hand rather than waiting for it.
  3. Continuity from B3+ to P+. On a common-port board these are almost always the same copper, given two pads only so the pack wire and the load wire each get their own. Confirm it: if they read as one node, the wire into B3+ carries the whole load current and must be thick. If they read open, B3+ is sense-only and the load takes P+ instead.
A shorted balance lead on a 12P group is a fire, not a spark Twelve parallel 18650s behind a 24 AWG wire will make that wire glow before anything gives up. Sleeve the taps, strain-relieve them where they leave the pack, and solder them last. Take the old board off one lead at a time, insulating each as it comes free, and never let the B− lead touch B3+.

Connect the taps in this order — B− first B− → B1+ → B2+ → B3+. The protection ICs are powered from the taps themselves. Land a high tap before the ones below it and a single stage sees most of the pack voltage across inputs rated for one cell, and it dies silently — leaving a board that looks fine and protects nothing.

The safe way is to make it impossible to get wrong: solder the four leads to the pack first with the board disconnected, then meter the free connector — B−→B1+, B1+→B2+ and B2+→B3+ each about 4.07 V, and B−→B3+ about 12.22 V — and only then plug it into the board. One plug, one order, nothing left to sequence by hand.

03 What this changes in the rest of the rig

The star point moved — this is the change most likely to be got wrong In Rev 2.0 every ground landed on the battery's negative terminal. In Rev 3.0 they all land on P−. A return wired to B− instead has two consequences, and the second is the nasty one: that load is unprotected, and it keeps draining the pack after the BMS has cut off — straight past the very cut-off meant to save the cells from over-discharge.

The rule that makes it checkable: exactly one wire in the rig touches B−, and it is the one coming from the pack. If you can see two, one of them is wrong.

04 The acceptance test

This is deliberately the same measurement that diagnosed the original fault. Had it been run when the 4S board went on, none of the last week's debugging would have happened.

  1. B− to P− must read a few millivolts — just the FETs' on-resistance. If it reads about 0.55 V, both FETs are off and current is sneaking through the body diodes. That is the exact 4S symptom, and it means the board is latched, not working.
  2. P+ to P− must equal B3+ to B− within a few millivolts. A large difference is the same fault seen from the other side.
  3. Each group within 50 mV of the others — B−→B1+, B1+→B2+, B2+→B3+.
  4. Only then close S1, and only with the motor uncoupled from the head.
What good looks like B−→P− a few mV · P+→P− ≈ 12.2 V · three groups at 4.07 V each · S1 open still reads 12.2 V at P+. Then, and only then, is anything measured on battery worth trusting.

06 Bring-up order

Unchanged from Rev 2.0 except that it now starts at the pack. Each step is provable before the next one can damage anything.

  1. Charger measured open-circuit, groups measured, old 4S board off.
  2. Taps soldered to the pack, connector metered, then plugged into the BMS.
  3. The acceptance test in 04. Nothing below this line is meaningful until it passes.
  4. Star-point every return onto P−, then check continuity from the Pi's header pin 39 all the way back to P− before any supply is connected.
  5. Converter on the bench, no load: confirm U3 holds 5.1 V from the real pack.
  6. Power the Pi only. Panel reachable, nothing else wired.
  7. Fit R_PU, then the three series resistors, then the signal wires. With the driver powered and the Pi off, measure ENABLE — it must sit at VCC, not near zero.
  8. Motor uncoupled from the head. Run a scan, press Stop mid-move, confirm it halts.
  9. Measure M+ while the motor is turning — with U6 gone it should now hold up near pack voltage instead of sagging.
Still open, and not fixed by this board S1's DC rating is unconfirmed. It breaks a DC inductive load, DC arcs do not self-extinguish the way AC ones do, and an under-rated switch slowly welds its contacts. A welded emergency stop looks fine right up until it is needed.
