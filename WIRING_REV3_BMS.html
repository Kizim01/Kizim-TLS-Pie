Rev 3.1 — the pack is 4S3P, and the BMS was right

The pack is 12 cells in 4 rows of 3, not the 36 the record claimed. That one fact overturns the diagnosis: the 4S board already fitted is the correct part, the pack really was flat, and the fix is a charger — not a new BMS.

Rev3.1 Supersedes3.0 — assumed 3S Date2026-08-11 Pack4S3P · ~130 Wh ERC0 violations

00 What changed, and why it matters

The BMS was never the fault A 4S BMS on a 4S pack is the right part doing its job. At the measured 12.22 V a 4S pack sits at 3.05 V per cell — genuinely low — and a group dragged under the cutoff latches it off. The ~0.55 V across B− to P− was correct behaviour, not a defect. Do not fit the 3S board. Charge the pack instead.

01 What to actually do now

  1. Measure the charger open-circuit, before anything. A 4S Li-ion pack charges to 16.8 V. A 12.6 V supply is a 3S charger and will never fill this pack past about half. 13.8–14.4 V is a lead-acid unit — harmless here at 3.45–3.6 V/cell, but it undercharges. This inverts the earlier warning: on a 3S pack that same charger would have been dangerous; on 4S it is merely inadequate.
  2. Charge the pack. The BMS should release as the charger comes up. If it does not release after a proper 16.8 V charge, then the board is suspect.
  3. Measure the four groups. B−→B1+, B1+→B2+, B2+→B3+, B3+→B4+ — each about 4.2 V when full and within 50 mV of the others. The weak group shows up here, and it is the one that tripped the cutoff. Three parallel cells means one tired cell drags a whole group.
  4. Then re-check B− to P−. It should now read a few millivolts. ~0.55 V after a full charge is the board failing; ~0.55 V while flat is the board working.
  5. Check the VLP-16's input range before closing S2. See the warning below — this is the one place going to 4S makes things worse.
New on 4S: S2 hands the lidar up to 16.8 V S2 passes raw pack voltage straight to the VLP-16's interface box. Under the old 3S assumption that leg never exceeded 12.6 V. A fully charged 4S pack is 16.8 V. Check the sensor's own stated input range before that switch is ever closed — if it will not take 16.8 V, that leg needs a regulator. Everything else on the rig is comfortable: the Big Easy Driver takes 8–35 V on M+ (and more volts is more torque), and the LM2596 takes 4.5–40 V in.
A shorted balance lead on a 3P group is still a fire Three parallel 18650s behind a 24 AWG wire will make that wire glow. If you disturb the taps at all: sleeve them, strain-relieve them where they leave the pack, work one lead at a time, and never let B− touch B4+.

02 The wiring rules that still stand

These were right in Rev 3.0 and survive the correction unchanged — they follow from the BMS having its FETs in the negative leg, which is true of the 4S board too.

Every ground lands on P−, never on B− A return wired to B− bypasses the protection FETs, so that load is unprotected — and it keeps draining the pack after the BMS has cut off, straight past the cut-off meant to save the cells. On a pack that has already been run down once, that is exactly the wire that turns a flat pack into a dead one.

The checkable form: exactly one wire in the rig touches B−, and it is the one from the pack. If you can see two, one of them is wrong.
Separate port: C− is not P− This board has a C− pad, so charge and discharge have separate returns. The charger's negative goes to C−; the rig's ground goes to P−. Do not bond them — that defeats the separate charge and discharge FETs, which is the entire purpose of the pad. (The 3S board bought by mistake has no C− at all, which is what made it common-port.)

03 The schematic — one page, every conductor drawn

Nothing on this sheet is joined by name. There are no net labels doing the connecting: the rails are real horizontal wires, the drops are real vertical wires, and every junction dot is a real node. A crossing without a dot is genuinely not a connection — which is what lets unrelated nets cross freely and still leaves the page unambiguous. The ten rail names are a reading aid; delete every one and the netlist is identical.
